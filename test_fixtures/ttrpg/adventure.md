=== Part One: Above the Water

# Introduction

*The Drowned Bell* is a short adventure for three to five characters of 3rd level. It takes one or two sessions to play. The characters are hired to find out why the bell of a flooded abbey has started ringing again, and what has been coming up out of the marsh on the nights it rings.

Everything in this book is written for the person running the game. Boxed text is meant to be read aloud or paraphrased to the players; everything else is for your eyes only.

~~~ sidebar title="Running This Adventure"
You need the core rules of a fifth-edition fantasy game. Creatures whose statistics appear in this book are given in full where they first appear; the others use the statistics of an ordinary creature of the same kind.

If your group is larger or stronger, add one bog-thrall to each fight in Chapter 2 and give the Abbess her lair actions from the first round rather than the second.
~~~

## Background

Forty years ago the Abbey of Saint Wenna stood on an island of firm ground in the Mere. When the sea wall at Hollin failed, the Mere rose in a single winter and the abbey drowned with its bell still hanging in the tower. The sisters left by boat. One did not.

Sister Orla, the abbey's bell-warden, refused to leave the relics in the crypt. The water took her with them. Something in the crypt kept her, after a fashion, and for forty years she has kept it in turn. This spring a peat-cutter's spade broke open a sluice that had been sealed since the flood, the water in the crypt began to fall, and Orla woke.

## Adventure Hooks

Choose one of the hooks below, or let each character take a different one.

~~~ table
d4 | Hook
1 | **The reeve's coin.** Reeve Tamsin Aldous offers 50 gp to anyone who will row out to the abbey and stop the bell.
2 | **A missing brother.** One character's sibling, a peat-cutter, did not come home three nights ago.
3 | **The scholar.** A sage in Hollin pays 100 gp for a rubbing of the inscription on the bell.
4 | **The dream.** A character dreams of a woman in grey who rings a bell under water, and wakes with marsh mud on their boots.
~~~

# Chapter 1: Hollin

The village of Hollin crouches behind what is left of its sea wall: forty houses of tarred timber on stilts, a smokehouse, a chapel with no priest, and the Lantern, an inn that leans noticeably toward the Mere.

~~~ readaloud
Rain drives in sideways off the water. Every house has its shutters closed, though it is barely past noon, and a string of small iron bells has been hung across the only street. They clink in the wind. Somewhere far out over the reeds, slow and patient, a much larger bell answers them.
~~~

## H1. The Lantern

The innkeeper, Bran Coyle, is a big, soft-spoken man who lost a hand to a pike as a boy and has never let anyone help him lift a barrel since. He knows everyone in Hollin and will tell the characters what the village knows, which is less than it thinks it knows.

~~~ list
The bell first rang eleven nights ago, at the turn of the tide.
Three people have gone missing since: two peat-cutters and a girl who went looking for one of them.
Something has been leaving tracks in the mud below the sea wall: bare feet, very long toes, always walking *toward* the village.
Nobody has rowed out to the abbey in thirty years. The channel through the reeds has silted, and only Old Hesk still knows it.
~~~

## H2. Old Hesk's Hut

Hesk lives alone at the end of the causeway with a great many cats. She will guide the characters through the reeds for 10 gp, or for nothing if someone brings back her husband's ring from the abbey crypt, where he was buried before the flood.

~~~ readaloud
The hut smells of smoke and fish oil. An old woman with a clay pipe watches you from a chair made of driftwood. "You'll be wanting the channel," she says, before anyone has spoken. "Everyone wants the channel, once the bell starts."
~~~

~~~ sidebar title="Hesk Knows More Than She Says"
Hesk was a novice at the abbey before the flood, and she was the last person to see Sister Orla alive. She will not volunteer this. A character who succeeds on a DC 15 Wisdom (Insight) check notices that she flinches whenever the bell rings, and that she never once calls the abbess by name.

If the characters press her kindly, she tells them the truth: Orla was not left behind. Orla *sent the boats away* and barred the crypt door from the inside.
~~~

## H3. Below the Sea Wall

If the characters wait below the sea wall after dark, two bog-thralls climb out of the Mere at the turn of the tide and walk toward the village. They are not hostile unless attacked or blocked; they are looking for the iron bells, which they tear down and carry back into the water.

~~~ statblock name="Bog-Thrall" meta="Medium undead, unaligned"
Armor Class: 9
Hit Points: 26 (4d8 + 8)
Speed: 25 ft., swim 30 ft.
STR 14 | DEX 8 | CON 15 | INT 3 | WIS 8 | CHA 5
Saving Throws: Wis +1
Damage Immunities: poison
Condition Immunities: exhausted, poisoned
Senses: darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 9
Languages: understands the languages it knew in life but can't speak
Challenge: 1/2 (100 XP)
Proficiency Bonus: +2
--- Traits
***Peat-Preserved.*** When the bog-thrall is reduced to 0 hit points, it makes a Constitution saving throw with a DC of 5 + the damage taken, unless the damage is fire or radiant. On a success, it drops to 1 hit point instead.
***Called by the Bell.*** While the abbey bell is ringing, the bog-thrall can't be frightened or charmed.
--- Actions
***Grasping Hands.*** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. *Hit:* 6 (1d8 + 2) bludgeoning damage, and the target is grappled (escape DC 12).
***Drag Under.*** The bog-thrall moves up to its swim speed into water, pulling a creature it is grappling with it. The creature can't breathe while it remains submerged.
~~~

=== Part Two: Below the Water

# Chapter 2: The Drowned Abbey

The abbey rises out of the reeds like a ship that sank by the stern. The chapter house and cloister are underwater; the church nave is waist-deep; the bell tower stands clear of the water, its top floor open to the sky.

~~~ readaloud
The channel opens without warning and the abbey is simply *there*, much closer than you expected, much larger. Green water laps at the sills of tall windows. A heron lifts off the roof of the nave. And up in the tower, though there is no wind at all, the bell is swinging.
~~~

## General Features

Unless a room says otherwise, the abbey has the following features.

~~~ table
Feature | Description
Light | Dim light by day through the high windows; darkness at night.
Water | The nave and lower rooms are difficult terrain. Creatures without a swim speed have disadvantage on melee attacks while waist-deep.
Walls | Mortared stone, slick with weed. Climbing them needs a DC 15 Strength (Athletics) check.
Sound | The bell can be heard everywhere in the abbey. While it rings, Wisdom (Perception) checks that rely on hearing have disadvantage.
~~~

## A1. The West Door

The great west door hangs off one hinge. Three skiffs, rotten through, are tied to its ring.

## A2. The Nave

Pews float upside down in the nave like the hulls of small boats. Four bog-thralls stand motionless among them, facing the altar, waist-deep in the water. They turn toward any light.

~~~ sidebar title="Variant: A Peaceful Approach"
The bog-thralls are not cruel. They are the drowned of Hollin, and they are doing what the bell tells them. A character who rings a handbell, or strikes metal in time with the abbey bell, can walk among them unmolested for as long as they keep the rhythm, with a DC 12 Charisma (Performance) check each round.
~~~

## A3. The Bell Tower

A spiral stair climbs the inside of the tower. The bell itself hangs in the open belfry at the top: bronze gone green, as tall as a person, with a rope of braided reed that runs down through a hole in the floor to the crypt far below.

~~~ readaloud
Words are cast around the bell's shoulder in letters as long as your hand. Someone has scraped the green away from them recently, and they shine.

*Whoever hears me, wake. Whoever wakes, keep.*
~~~

## A4. The Crypt

The crypt is the only part of the abbey still entirely under water, and it is falling. When the characters arrive, the water stands at chest height and drops a few inches every hour.

At the far end, on the reliquary, sits the thing that was Sister Orla.

~~~ statblock name="Orla, the Drowned Abbess" meta="Medium undead, lawful neutral"
Armor Class: 15 (corroded mail)
Hit Points: 97 (13d8 + 39)
Speed: 30 ft., swim 40 ft.
STR 16 | DEX 13 | CON 16 | INT 12 | WIS 17 | CHA 18
Saving Throws: Con +6, Wis +6
Skills: Insight +6, Religion +4
Damage Resistances: cold; bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing from nonmagical attacks
Damage Immunities: poison
Condition Immunities: charmed, exhausted, frightened, poisoned
Senses: truesight 30 ft., passive Perception 13
Languages: Common, Celestial
Challenge: 5 (1,800 XP)
Proficiency Bonus: +3
--- Traits
***Bound to the Bell.*** Orla can't willingly move more than 300 feet from the abbey bell. If the bell is destroyed, she becomes vulnerable to radiant damage.
***Drowned Grace.*** While Orla is in water, any creature that starts its turn within 10 feet of her must succeed on a DC 14 Constitution saving throw or lose its breath until the start of its next turn.
***Spellcasting.*** Orla casts the following spells, using Wisdom as her spellcasting ability (spell save DC 14), requiring no material components:
At will: *chill touch*, *thaumaturgy*
2/day each: *bane*, *hold person*
1/day: *bestow curse*
--- Actions
***Multiattack.*** Orla makes two Bell-Rope attacks.
***Bell-Rope.*** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +6 to hit, reach 15 ft., one target. *Hit:* 10 (2d6 + 3) bludgeoning damage plus 7 (2d6) cold damage. If the target is Large or smaller, it is pulled up to 10 feet toward Orla.
***Toll (Recharge 5–6).*** Orla pulls the bell-rope. Each creature of her choice within 60 feet that can hear the bell must make a DC 14 Wisdom saving throw, taking 18 (4d8) thunder damage and being frightened until the end of its next turn on a failure, or half as much damage on a success.
--- Reactions
***Keep.*** When a creature within 15 feet of Orla tries to leave the crypt, Orla makes one Bell-Rope attack against it.
--- Lair Actions
On initiative count 20 (losing initiative ties), Orla can take one of the following lair actions; she can't use the same one two rounds in a row:
The water in the crypt surges. Each creature in the water must succeed on a DC 13 Strength saving throw or be knocked prone.
The bell tolls once on its own. Each bog-thrall in the abbey can use its reaction to move up to its speed.
Drowned voices whisper from the walls. One creature Orla can see has disadvantage on its next saving throw.
~~~

# Chapter 3: Conclusion

If Orla is destroyed while the bell still hangs, she re-forms in the crypt at the next turn of the tide. To end her for good, the characters must either take the bell down and carry it out of the Mere, or ring it themselves, once, and say the words on its shoulder aloud: *whoever wakes, keep*. A character who does so takes up Orla's watch, and the drowned of Hollin go quiet.

~~~ sidebar title="Rewards"
Reeve Aldous pays what she promised, and Hesk, if she gets her husband's ring back, gives the characters the abbey's last boat-key: a small brass key that opens any lock that has been under water.
~~~
