# The Lantern Keeper

The rain had not stopped for three days, and Aldren had begun to suspect it never would. He stood beneath the eaves of the Split Lantern Inn, watching the water carve small rivers through the mud of Briars Hollow, and thought about how far he had come to arrive at a place so determined to be forgotten.

Inside, someone was laughing. The sound carried oddly through the storm, warm and out of place, and for a moment he let himself imagine walking in and being no one in particular. A traveller. A man with dry boots and no history.

He pushed the door open.

* * *

The innkeeper looked up from behind the bar, and her expression did the thing that all their expressions did eventually. The flicker of recognition. The careful arrangement of the face into something neutral.

"We're full," she said.

"You're not," Aldren answered. "I can see the room behind you, and I can count."

She set down the glass she had been pretending to clean. The lantern above her swung gently, though there was no wind inside, and its light split into two as it always did here, throwing a double shadow across the floor.

# The Second Night

Morning, such as it was, arrived grey and grudging. Aldren had slept badly. The dreams again, the ones with the dark water and the voice beneath it, and he woke with the particular exhaustion of a man who has rested without resting.

He found her already awake, sitting by the cold hearth with her hands wrapped around a cup that had long since stopped steaming.

"You talk in your sleep," she said, without turning around.

"So I've been told."

"You said a name." Now she did turn, and her eyes had the flat patience of someone who had decided to stop being afraid of answers. "You said *Marenne*."

The name landed in the room like a dropped plate. Aldren said nothing for a long moment, and the lantern overhead swung, and split, and threw its two shadows, and waited with them.

# Chapter Three · Correspondence

What he did not tell her was that Marenne had written. The letter had arrived three weeks before he came north, forwarded twice, corners soft from handling. He had read it until he could hear her voice in the words, and then he had stopped reading.

~~~ letter from="M. Aldren" to="The Keeper" date="14th of Ashmonth"
I found the letter you left under the lantern stone last autumn. I have kept it all this time without knowing why. I think I know now. If you are still in the Hollow, I am coming north before the snow.

Do not go anywhere on my account. But do not leave on my account either.

— M.
~~~

He had read it standing at the kitchen table, coat still on, bag still in his hand. He had set it down carefully, as if it might break, and then gone to stand at the window for a long time.

* * *

She had written again after that. This one was shorter.

~~~ journal date="Thursday, 19th Ashmonth" author="M. Aldren"
I told no one I was leaving. There did not seem to be anyone left to tell. The house is very quiet now that Father is gone, and quiet has stopped feeling like peace and started feeling like something that needs to be explained.

I think I have been waiting to do this for a long time. I think the Hollow has been waiting too.
~~~

The journal had not been meant for him. He had found it tucked inside the letter, a single torn page, and he understood from the handwriting alone that she had included it by mistake. He had not mentioned it. He had kept it.

* * *

On the twelfth day there was a telegram. He had not known she knew where to send one.

~~~ telegram to="Aldren, Split Lantern Inn, Briars Hollow"
ARRIVING EVENING COACH STOP THREE DAYS HENCE STOP DO NOT WORRY STOP I AM NOT WORRIED STOP
~~~

He read it three times. He was not sure which part he believed.

* * *

A week later, the valley paper ran a notice. He found it folded into his door.

~~~ newspaper headline="LANTERN FOUND UNLIT FOR THIRD CONSECUTIVE NIGHT" date="22 Ashmonth" source="The Briars Hollow Courier"
Concern is growing among residents of the eastern valley following three successive nights in which the landmark lantern above the Split Lantern Inn was found dark. The light, which has served as a waymark for travellers crossing the high moor since the time of the third rebuilding, went unlit on the evening of the 19th and has not been restored. The innkeeper was unavailable for comment. The origin of the disruption remains unexplained.
~~~

Aldren had not lit it. He had meant to. But each evening he had gone up to the roof with the oil and the matches and stood there instead, looking north toward the coach road in the dark.

* * *

They found the file in the morning, slipped beneath the door of the back room with no indication of who had left it or when.

~~~ redacted classification="RESTRICTED — ARCHIVE DIVISION, INQUIRY BRANCH"
Subject entered the valley on or about the 7th of Ashmonth. No prior record of residence. Identity confirmed through secondary documentation as of the 12th. Associations: one known correspondent, currently travelling north from the southern lowlands. Purpose of visit: undetermined.

Recommend continued observation. File to remain open.
~~~

Aldren set it on the table beside the lamp and sat down and looked at it for a long time.

He had thought, coming here, that he was done being watched. He was beginning to think that was not how it worked.
